# Human Docs

Write for the reader who needs to use, operate, or change the system.

## Principles

- State what the system does before explaining internals.
- Prefer concrete verbs, short sentences, and familiar terms.
- Separate confirmed facts from assumptions and examples.
- Include prerequisites, commands, expected outcomes, and failure recovery when they help a reader complete a task.
- Keep examples runnable and label placeholders clearly.

## Workflow

1. Identify the reader and the task they need to complete.
2. Verify behavior against the source, configuration, or a repeatable command.
3. Draft the shortest structure that answers what, why, and how.
4. Add examples only when they clarify a decision or action.
5. Review for accuracy, stale details, undefined terms, and unnecessary jargon.

## Style checks

- Use headings that describe the reader's goal.
- Put warnings next to the action they affect.
- Link to authoritative references instead of duplicating long material.
- Do not claim results that have not been verified.
